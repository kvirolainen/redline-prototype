# Concept

## The problem

Clutch wants more gross profit: profit per car × cars sold. Cars sold is capped by supply, not demand. Clutch can sell more than it buys today, and any surplus can go out wholesale. **Redline is a supply play:** buy used cars from dealerships, not only from individuals.

Dealerships have cars to give. A dealer's business is new cars and service, and trade-ins are just the price of closing a new-car deal. Every trade-in the dealer won't retail is a chore:

- **Pricing it.** The sales manager haggles at the desk and then asks the sales director to sign off. Get it wrong and the dealer loses money.
- **Getting rid of it.** Dealer-only wholesale auctions mean transport, listing and weeks of waiting.
- **What it fetches.** Auction prices are often poor.

To win, Redline has to be clearly better than the auction: **a better price, cash sooner, and no logistics.**

## What we learned in the room

From the whiteboard session with Clutch:

- **More cars is always good.** Too much inventory isn't a worry: surplus is wholesaled.
- **Two people decide at the dealer.** The sales manager prices the trade-in. The sales director approves it, decides retail vs wholesale, and would sign a Redline contract.
- **Clutch already has most of the parts:** the instant offer individuals get, the same 15-minute inspection at pickup, and a team that already sells wholesale cars to dealers.
- **Tesla is the closest example.** Clutch already makes offers on Tesla trade-ins, and Tesla passes them to the customer.
- **Feedback on the first sketches:** keep the summary bar slim, and open the car table on *awaiting pickup*.

## The idea in one line

At the desk, the sales manager gets Clutch's offer instead of haggling. Clutch picks the car up on the dealer's regular pickup day, checks it against what was declared and pays. If the check finds a difference, the car is re-priced and the director decides.

## What's in this prototype

One car, followed across four people:

| Who | Screen |
|---|---|
| Maria, Sales Manager | **Trade-in**: car details → offer |
| Jordan, Clutch ops | **Ops board**: pickup runs and what's stuck |
| Sam, Clutch inspector | **Inspector** (phone): check vs declared → confirm, re-price or not ready |
| Dave, Sales Director | **Back office**: the dealer's cars, re-price decisions, value vs auction |

**Faked on purpose:** pricing (a stub where Clutch's calculator plugs in), sign-in (a person switcher), payments and notifications.
