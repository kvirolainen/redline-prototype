# Desk or online?

A trade-in needs a price before the new-car deal can close. Redline could give that price in two places: **at the dealer's desk**, during the visit, or **online**, before the customer walks in. The choice decides who looks at the screen, when the price gets settled, and how much the dealer has to change how they work.

## A. At the desk

The sales manager runs Clutch's offer with the customer sitting there.

- ✅ **The dealer's process stays the same.** Only the haggling is replaced. Easy to pilot with one dealer.
- ✅ **A firm number ends the negotiation**, right when the new-car deal is being closed.
- ⚠️ **One screen, two audiences.** The customer should see the offer. The dealer's own numbers (the offer vs auction, what to declare) shouldn't be in front of them.
- ⚠️ **The customer watches the price move.** Every condition answer becomes something to argue about.
- ⚠️ **If the calculator can't price the car on the spot**, the moment is lost.

## B. Online, before the visit

The customer gets a Clutch quote at home and brings it to any participating dealer, who honours it and hands the car to Clutch.

- ✅ **The haggling happens before the visit.** The desk only checks the quote and applies it.
- ✅ **It fits patterns that already exist.** Tesla passes Clutch's offer to its customers. Kelley Blue Book's Instant Cash Offer in Canada works this way with participating dealers.
- ✅ **The customer still has a reason to go through the dealer.** In Ontario a trade-in at the dealer lowers the HST on the new car (13% of $17,850 is about $2,300). A direct sale to Clutch doesn't.
- ⚠️ **Bigger change for dealers.** The price is set outside their store, and some will see that as losing control of the deal.
- ⚠️ **Clutch gains a consumer channel it has to market**, and needs dealers signed up before the quote is worth anything.

## What this prototype does

**A for now, built so B can follow.**

- The trade-in screen keeps the customer's view clean: the car and Clutch's offer. The dealer's comparison sits on Maria's side only.
- The offer is a record with an expiry, not a screen state. A quote made online could arrive at the desk the same way, and everything after it (pickup, inspection, payment) doesn't change.
- If the calculator needs more time, Maria gets "firm offer by 3 p.m." instead of a promise Clutch can't stand behind.
