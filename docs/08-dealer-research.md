# Dealer research

Who should be the first Redline dealer, and can public data find them?

**Short answer:** public data shows *what kind* of dealer to call first, not *which one*. It can't show who sells how many cars at auction. EBlock and OPENLANE show that only to dealers.

## What public data shows

- **Auction is a weak exit.** On a given week, only about 45–70% of cars run through Canadian auction lanes sell ([CBB weekly, 2026](https://www.canadianblackbook.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/CBB_MarketInsights_06302026.pdf)). The rest get re-run, with more fees and transport, or go back to the lot.
- **Each sale costs money and time.** In the US, auction charges average about $385 a car, and transport adds $100–200. OPENLANE Canada sell fees drop with volume, so a small seller pays the most. Payment comes once the ownership papers clear. No public Canadian all-in figure exists. A placeholder of **C$400–800 a car** needs checking with a dealer.
- **Dealers keep most trade-ins and sell the rest at auction.** US franchise dealers keep 85–87% overall ([vAuto, 2026](https://news.dealershipguy.com/p/trade-in-retention-rates-hit-87-in-vauto-data)). In one 10-store US group, luxury stores kept 75–85% and Kia stores only about 50%. No Canadian figure is public.
- **It's a small number of cars per store.** Rough estimate: **5–10 cars a month** per dealership go to auction. It mixes Canadian used-sales volumes per store with US keep rates. That's enough to test price and process with one dealer, but scale means many dealers or a group.
- **Prices exist, sellers don't.** OPENLANE publishes monthly average auction prices by model, e.g. RAV4 $26,009 and Civic $16,694 in March 2026 ([OPENLANE](https://www.openlane.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Canadian_Market_Insights_MAR26.pdf)). Canadian Black Book sells wholesale values by VIN and km. Neither names the dealers selling the cars.

## Who to call first

| Dealer type | Cars they send to auction | Fit for Clutch | First deal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Luxury store, owner-run** (BMW, Mercedes, Lexus, Acura, Volvo) | Fewer cars. Mainstream trade-ins of other brands, and own-brand cars past the certified pre-owned limit (about 6 yrs / 120k km at Mercedes) | ✅ 3–9 years old, mainstream: Clutch's range | ✅ One decision-maker. Clutch doesn't compete with their certified pre-owned stock |
| **EV-only brands** (Rivian, Lucid) | Nearly every trade-in. No used lot in Canada | ✅ Mainstream cars of every brand | ⚠️ Corporate deal, low volume, but Tesla is the template |
| **Mainstream import store** (Hyundai, Kia, Nissan) | Most cars | ⚠️ More old, high-km, rough cars | ⚠️ More "you'll cherry-pick" worry |
| **Big groups** (AutoCanada, Dilawri, Humberview…) | Many, but they move them between their own stores first | Same cars | ❌ Already have an exit: AutoCanada owns North Toronto Auction, and several groups signed exclusive ADESA/TradeRev deals in 2017 |
| **Independent used lots** | Few | | ❌ They buy at auction more than they sell |

**Pick: one owner-run luxury store.** Clutch's wholesale desk already sells the cars Clutch doesn't keep, so Redline can take a dealer's whole batch. That answers the cherry-picking worry.

## Leads in Toronto and Mississauga

From CarGurus dealer pages (11 Nov 2026, small samples of about 24 used cars each). Old, high-km and off-brand stock on a new-car lot hints at trade-ins with no good exit. It's a lead, not proof.

- **Streetsville Hyundai, Mississauga:** 13 of 24 used cars are other brands, 9 are 2017 or older, 12 are over 120k km. No parent group found.
- **Rexdale Hyundai, Etobicoke:** 529 vehicles listed, 14 of 22 used cars are other brands. Says it was Ontario's #1 Hyundai retailer by units in 2021.
- **Yorkdale Toyota, Mississauga Honda:** a wide spread of luxury and EV trade-ins on a mainstream lot.
- **Rivian (Toronto, Vaughan) and Lucid (Yorkdale, Vaughan):** take trade-ins of most other brands, no Canadian used lot.
- **Luxury stores look the other way:** Parkview BMW, Lexus On The Park and Audi Mississauga show young, low-km, mostly own-brand used lots, with 0–1 mainstream 2017+ cars each. That fits the idea that their off-brand trade-ins leave by auction. Listings can't prove it, which is why the luxury pick needs the calls below. BMW Autohaus (Thornhill) is the exception: it retails old trade-ins of mixed brands, like Streetsville.

## How we'd make the list real

1. **Start with Clutch's own data.** The Dealer & Wholesale team works these auctions every week. Rank the GTA dealers it sees sending the most cars.
2. **Call 10 used-car managers** from the list above with three questions: how many trade-ins a month go to auction, what they net per car after fees, and how many days until they're paid.
3. **Price off Canadian Black Book wholesale by VIN**, so the trade-in screen's "beats auction by $X" is a real number.
4. **Agree on the scorecard before the first deal:** $ above auction, re-price rate, days to cash. The Back office **Results** tab already tracks the first two.
